# Requirements Conventions

- Ask one question at a time during interviews — do not present a list of questions all at once
- Summarize-and-confirm after gathering a cluster of related answers before moving on
- Use progressive depth — start with high-level "what and why" questions, drill into details only for areas the user signals are important
- Early in the interview, ask if there's an existing system being replaced or a competitor product to reference
- Proactively raise concerns the user may not have considered (auth edge cases, data migration, rate limiting, error recovery, accessibility)
- If a topic is clearly not applicable to the project, skip it — note it in out-of-scope rather than forcing coverage
- 🔧 Explicitly separate MVP scope from full vision in every requirements specification
- Identify stakeholders: decision-makers, end users, and who needs to sign off
- Define project-level success criteria distinct from per-requirement acceptance criteria
- Every requirement must be testable, actionable, and unambiguous
- Every requirement must have measurable acceptance criteria
- 🔧 Prioritize every requirement as must-have, should-have, or nice-to-have
- Build a glossary of domain-specific terms as they come up during the interview
- Record key decisions with their reasoning as they happen
- Flag risks and tensions between requirements immediately when noticed
- For bug fix requirements, focus on the root cause and define regression criteria — not just the reported symptom
- 🔧 Use sequential IDs for requirements (REQ-XXX) and personas (PERSONA-XXX)
